# Nomination guidelines

Nominating a study says its finding matters enough to look at again, through a
fresh replication or a reproduction of the original analysis. It is not an
accusation or a criticism of the authors.

Please keep nominations impersonal and focused on the work, never the authors.

## A good nomination

- **Names the study clearly.** Give the DOI so everyone is looking at the same
  paper. For the rare paper without one, give the full reference and a link.
- **Explains why revisiting is valuable**, in neutral terms. For example:
  - the finding is highly cited or informs practice or policy,
  - later work depends on it holding up,
  - the result is surprising, or the evidence base is thin,
  - open data or code make a reproduction feasible.
- **Says what kind of second look fits.** A *replication* collects new data with
  the same method. A *reproduction* re-runs the original analysis on the original
  data, and/or varies how the analysis is done to check the results are robust.
  For simulation and other computational studies, which have no data of their
  own, a reproduction re-runs or re-implements the code.
- **Notes what's openly available** (data, code, materials, preregistration, or
  an open-access version), so others can judge how feasible the work is.

## Please avoid

- Language that judges the researchers rather than the evidence.
- Claims of misconduct or fraud. This registry is not the place for allegations.
- Duplicates. Search the registry first; if the study is already listed, upvote
  it instead.

## What happens next

A maintainer checks each nomination against the Code of Conduct. Nominations are
visible straight away, marked "Pending review" until then. Anyone can upvote a
nomination, predict whether the finding will hold up, or join the team doing
the work.
